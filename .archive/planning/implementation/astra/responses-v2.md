## Remaining High/Blocker issues and merge advice

**Plan status:** every received OA finding has a written disposition and location. I retain no known unresolved High/Blocker plan-contract omission. This is author self-assessment for synthesis, not independent verification or approval. Four High feasibility gates remain in plan-v2 §10: Wayland delivery-time evidence/setup; independently scheduled abort and ownership-safe release; recognition/freshness with durable evidence; and CLI isolation/login/images plus useful fresh Jev decisions. Required capability failures block release and return to the user. D1–D8 remain unapproved choices as applicable; neither plan can settle them by wording.

Three merge recommendations/tradeoffs:

1. **Merge the evidence/permit and lifecycle contracts before selecting helper language.** Keep TypeScript engine and isolated native guard; compare C++ and Rust as out-of-process candidates. Memory safety, binding maintenance and packaging are tradeoffs to demonstrate, not reasons to force either preference now.
2. **Merge durability and logical side-effect identity with a gated small initial output scope.** Create-new modes reduce reconciliation complexity; richer modes or SQLite may be worthwhile but require explicit semantics, proof and user selection. Do not imply editor undo reverses external effects.
3. **Merge the finer dependency graph with strict dual-session/provider closure.** X11 development previews can precede successful Wayland work, but cannot complete the release. CLI authoring-only scope is a proposal; runtime Jev, Settings and completed required adapters stay in closure. Setup friction, semantic freshness and unknown-outcome limitations must remain visible at user gates.
